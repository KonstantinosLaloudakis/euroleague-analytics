Player Metrics

  • RAPM Player ML Model
    Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus — The gold standard for measuring individual impact. Uses ridge regression on 11,000+ game stints (contiguous stretches where the same 10 players are on court) to isolate each player's contribution, controlling for teammates and opponents simultaneously.

    Our implementation uses a two-pass informed prior: a first pass generates noisy RAPM estimates, then a box-score model (points, assists, steals, blocks, rebounds, turnovers, true shooting %) learns to predict RAPM from traditional stats. These predictions become the regularization center for the final pass — so low-minute players shrink toward their stats-based expectation rather than toward zero. This is the same concept behind ESPN's RPM and FiveThirtyEight's RAPTOR.

    Split into O-RAPM (offensive impact) and D-RAPM (defensive impact, positive = good defender). Measured in points per 100 possessions.
  • WPA Player
    Win Probability Added — Evaluates every play-by-play event through the lens of win probability. Each action (made shot, turnover, steal) shifts the game's win probability — WPA sums these shifts to measure who contributed most to actual wins. Rewards clutch performances and high-leverage plays.
  • WIR Player
    Weighted Impact Rating — An efficiency-adjusted alternative to Euroleague's official PIR. Reweights box-score stats to better reflect modern basketball value: playmaking (1.5x), steals (1.8x), blocks (1.2x), while penalizing inefficiency (missed shots, turnovers). Normalized per 40 minutes.
  • TPM Player
    True Plus-Minus — Raw point differential when a player is on the floor, normalized per 40 minutes. Unlike RAPM, this is unadjusted — a bench player on Real Madrid will look better than they are. Compare with RAPM to see who the context adjustment helps or hurts.
  • MVP Score Player
    Composite MVP Ranking — A weighted blend of per-game performance (PIR), win probability added (WPA), team success (win %), consistency (inverse coefficient of variation), and clutch efficiency. Designed to approximate how the Euroleague MVP award is actually voted on.

Team Metrics

  • Adjusted Ratings Team
    KenPom-style Four Factors — Offensive and defensive efficiency adjusted for strength of schedule with recency weighting (exponential decay, 0.97 per round). Recent games count more than early-season games, so the ratings reflect current form — not just season averages. Includes regular season, play-in, and playoff games once played; postseason games carry full weight while older regular-season games gradually fade. Adj_Net = Adj_Off - Adj_Def, measured in points per game.
  • Elo Rating Team
    A rolling rating system adapted from chess. Each game updates both teams' ratings based on the result and pre-game expectation. Parameters: K-factor = 10, home court advantage = 50 Elo points, 75% carryover between seasons with regression to the mean. Spans all 20 seasons continuously.
  • Strength of Schedule Team
    Average win percentage of opponents faced. Used to adjust raw records — a 20-10 team that played the hardest schedule is more impressive than a 22-8 team with the easiest. Also computed for remaining games to identify favorable or brutal finishes.
  • Home Court Advantage Team
    Decomposed into six components: shooting boost, whistle advantage (free throw rate), ball security (turnover rate), offensive rebounding boost, defensive squeeze, and residual (crowd/travel effects). Computed per-team, not just league-wide.

Predictive Models

  • Win Probability Model ML Model
    A Gradient Boosting Machine trained on historical play-by-play data with Platt scaling calibration. Predicts real-time win probability from four features: current score margin, seconds remaining, Elo rating difference, and home/away status. Used to compute WPA for every play-by-play event and power the Game Replay visualizations.
    Fallback (when model unavailable):
    WP = 1 / (1 + exp(-0.8 * margin / sigma))
    sigma = 11 * sqrt(seconds_remaining / 2400)
  • Oracle Forecast ML Model
    Game-by-game win/loss predictions using Logistic Regression. Features include adjusted net rating differential, Elo difference, home/away net ratings, recent form differential, and strength of schedule difference. Produces win probabilities and predicted margins for upcoming rounds.
  • Monte Carlo Simulation Simulation
    Simulates the remainder of the season 10,000 times to generate probability distributions for final standings. Each team's strength (points per game vs an average team) is estimated with a Bayesian model: every game's margin is treated as home-court advantage (3 pts) plus the strength difference plus noise (sd 12 pts), starting from a preseason prior based on last season's Elo. The fit adjusts for opponent and venue, and early results are shrunk toward the prior — historically only about a third of a team's margin over its first four games carries over to the rest of the season. Each simulation first draws a plausible strength for every team, then plays out all remaining games, so uncertainty about how good a team really is flows into the odds. Simulated final standings are ranked with the end-of-season tiebreakers: head-to-head record among tied teams, then head-to-head point differential, then overall point differential. Outputs playoff probabilities (Top 4, Top 6, Top 10), projected win totals, and seed distributions for every team. Backtested on the 2016–2024 regular seasons for calibration (when it says 80%, it happens about 80% of the time).
  • RAPM Ridge Regression ML Model
    Two-pass ridge regression with cross-validated regularization (alpha selected from [0.01 ... 5000]). Pass 1 uses low regularization (alpha=100) to generate training targets for the box-score prior model. Pass 2 uses full cross-validated alpha with the prior as the regularization center. Sparse design matrix with ~335 player columns + HCA indicator, weighted by possession count per stint.

Game Control Metrics

The Game Control Index (GCI) analyzes Win Probability trajectories — the second-by-second likelihood of each team winning — to measure how teams win, not just whether they did. Built from WP replay data across every game this season.

  • Dominance Score Team Game
    Average distance of WP from 50% across all plays: mean(WP - 0.5). Positive means the home team controlled the game; negative means the away team did. Range: -0.5 to +0.5.
  • Control Duration Team Game
    Percentage of plays where the eventual winner held WP above 60%. 100% means wire-to-wire cruise control; lower values indicate a contested game.
  • Drama Index Game
    Total variation of the WP curve: sum of absolute WP changes between consecutive plays. Higher values indicate more momentum swings and lead changes.
  • Comeback Magnitude Game
    For wins: 1.0 - min(winner WP). Measures how deep a deficit the winning team overcame. A comeback from 10% WP scores 0.90.
  • Crunch-Time Swing Team Game
    Net WP change in the final 5 minutes of game clock (Q4 from 5:00 to buzzer, plus overtime). Positive means the team gained control in the clutch.
  • Killer Instinct Team
    Net WP change during Q4 plays when the team was leading (WP above 55%). Positive means the team extended their lead; negative means they surrendered it. Measures close-out ability — do they finish games or let opponents back in?
  • GCI Rating Team
    Weighted composite of Dominance (35%), Control Duration (25%), Crunch-Time Swing (20%), and Killer Instinct (20%), z-normalized to a 0–100 scale across the league. Higher = more dominant game control profile.

Historical GCI Trends

The GCI History page extends the Game Control Index across all 20 Euroleague seasons (2007–2026), covering 5,000+ games. The same six sub-metrics and GCI Rating formula are applied to each historical season independently, with z-normalization computed within each season (so a GCI of 70 in 2007 means the same relative dominance as 70 in 2024).

Teams are included in trend comparisons only if they have 3 or more seasons of data. The 10 "legacy" teams (Olympiacos, Barcelona, Real Madrid, Panathinaikos, Maccabi Tel Aviv, Anadolu Efes, Fenerbahce, Baskonia, EA7 Milano, Zalgiris) have been present in all 20 seasons. Era breakdowns use three periods: Expansion Era (2007–2015, 24 teams), Contraction Era (2016–2019, 16 teams), and Modern Era (2020–2026, 18–20 teams).

Player Career Pages

Every player who has appeared in the Euroleague since the 2007-08 season has a dedicated career profile page. Season-by-season statistics are sourced from the official Euroleague API via PlayerStats.get_player_stats_all_seasons, covering traditional stats in PerGame mode across all phases.

Metrics shown

  • PPG / RPG / APG / SPG / BPG / TPG — per-game averages from the official feed
  • 2P% / 3P% / FT% — two-point, three-point, and free-throw percentages
  • PIR — Performance Index Rating, Euroleague's official efficiency metric

Career totals use GP-weighted averages for rate statistics and true sums for games played. The PIR trend chart highlights the career-peak season and the current season. RAPM is computed only for the current season and is shown in other dashboards, not in the career table.

Player data is refreshed as part of the weekly pipeline. Historical season data is cached and only re-fetched with the --force flag.

All-Time Records

The All-Time Records page ranks every player across all 20 Euroleague seasons (2007–08 to 2026–27) for career leaders and single-season bests. Data comes from player_career_stats.json, which is refreshed weekly.

Career Leaders

Counting stats (points, assists, rebounds, steals) are accumulated totals computed as Σ(per_game_average × games_played) across all seasons. This rewards longevity alongside production. A minimum of 100 GP (adjustable) filters out very short careers. PIR uses a GP-weighted career average.

Single-Season Records

Each season entry for every player is ranked independently. A minimum of 15 GP per season filters out statistical anomalies from injury-shortened appearances.

Data Pipeline

The entire analytics suite updates via a single command (python refresh_all.py) that orchestrates the full pipeline:

1
Fetch latest data from Euroleague API
2
Parse game results and play-by-play events
3
Train ML models (Win Probability, Oracle)
4
Compute ratings (Adjusted, Elo, RAPM, WPA, WIR)
5
Run Monte Carlo simulation (10K iterations)
6
Generate visualizations and export dashboard data

Quick Glossary

  • Per 100 Possessions — Normalizing stats by possessions instead of games removes pace effects. A fast team that plays 75 possessions per game and a slow team that plays 65 become comparable.
  • Stint — A continuous stretch of play where the same 10 players (5 per team) are on court. Substitutions create new stints. RAPM is built on these.
  • Ridge Regression — A form of regularized regression that prevents overfitting by penalizing large coefficients. Essential for RAPM because many lineup combinations are seen only a few times.
  • Informed Prior — Instead of assuming every player starts at zero impact, we use box-score stats to set a reasonable starting estimate. The on-court data then adjusts from there.
  • True Shooting % — Points / (2 * (FGA + 0.44 * FTA)). Accounts for three-pointers and free throws in a single efficiency number.
  • Four Factors — Dean Oliver's framework: effective FG%, turnover rate, offensive rebound rate, and free throw rate. These four factors explain most of the variance in team success.

📋 Lineup Net Ratings

Lineup Net Ratings measures the combined impact of 5-man units and 2-man pairs on the scoreboard, expressed as points scored or allowed per 100 possessions while that group was on the floor together.

  • ORtg — Points scored per 100 possessions when the unit was on the floor.
  • DRtg — Points allowed per 100 possessions. Lower is better.
  • Net Rtg — ORtg minus DRtg. Positive means the unit outscored opponents.
  • Poss — Estimated possessions, using the standard formula: FGA + 0.44 × FTA + TO − OREB.
  • Minimum threshold: 75 possessions for 5-man lineups, 150 for pairs. Units below this are hidden to reduce small-sample noise.
  • Unlike RAPM, lineup ratings are raw — they do not adjust for the quality of opponents faced. A great lineup on a weak schedule will look better than it truly is.
